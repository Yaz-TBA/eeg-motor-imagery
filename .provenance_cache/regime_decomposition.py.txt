The post-cue row on its own proves nothing -- a model can look
above chance there because of subject leakage or drift that has
nothing to do with the cue. The pre-cue row is the control that
rules those out: it is matched in band, length and folds, and it
contains neither the cue flash nor any imagery. Chance pre-cue plus
above-chance post-cue localises the effect to cue onset, so regime
C's 'the ranking flips' is the CNN reading something time-locked to
the cue rather than learning motor imagery better than CSP does.
It does NOT say which post-cue thing: see the LIMITATION note next
to the cell definitions -- EEGBCI's cue is position-confounded with
the class, so cue flash and imagery onset cannot be separated here.

AND HALF THE PREDICTION THIS CELL WAS BUILT ON FAILED.
The cue-only cell was written to predict that EEGNet would score
above chance in the cue window and that CSP would NOT. CSP scores
53.7% there (p=0.023), which is above chance.
So CSP is not blind to whatever the cue window carries; it simply
follows it less than EEGNet does. The cue-locking conclusion above
stands, because it rests on the pre-cue control and the paired
deltas. The MECHANISM story -- 'CSP's log-variance features cannot
see a phase-locked evoked response' -- does not, and should not be
repeated anywhere downstream.

Every p in this block is a t-test across 20 leave-one-subject-out
folds treated as independent draws. They overlap in 19/20 of their
training data, so these p-values are anti-conservative. Read them as
ordering evidence, not as calibrated tail probabilities.
