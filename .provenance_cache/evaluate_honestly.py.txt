
45 trials (21 hands, 24 feet) | chance = 53.3%

--- 1. Quantization: a 20% test set is 9 trials ---
A fold's accuracy can only be k/9, i.e. steps of 11.1%.
Attainable values near the headline: 7/9=77.8%, 8/9=88.9%, 9/9=100.0%

The 10 folds produced 2 distinct values: 88.9%, 100.0%
Total correct across folds: 85/90

--- 2. Coverage: ShuffleSplit is not a partition ---
Test-set class balance per fold (hands, feet): [(6, 3), (5, 4), (2, 7), (7, 2), (4, 5), (2, 7), (2, 7), (4, 5), (3, 6), (5, 4)]
Trials never tested: 5 of 45
Times a trial is tested: min=0 max=6
Worst-balanced fold is (2, 7) -- guessing its majority alone scores 77.8%, not the 53.3% we print as chance.

--- 3. Stratified k-fold, where every trial is tested once ---
ShuffleSplit(10, 0.2)    94.4% +/- 5.6%
StratifiedKFold(5)       91.1% +/- 4.4%
StratifiedKFold(10)      92.5% +/- 16.0%
Note the +/- on 10-fold: 4-5 test trials per fold makes a fold std meaningless.

Running... (this is the slow part)

p-value           : <= 0.001  (the floor of a 1000-shuffle test, not a measurement)

Null is centred near 50%, so the reference distribution is well formed.
NOTE: p is bounded below by 1/(n+1) = 0.0010. Report this as p <= 0.001, not as a measured value.

The two estimators agree in expectation to +0.2 points (StratifiedKFold minus ShuffleSplit; positive means the estimator change RAISES the expectation).
So the headline drop from 94.4% to 91.1% is mostly SEED LUCK, not a
change of estimator. The estimator change is the right call for other
reasons (coverage, stratification); it just is not worth 3 points.
