--- The confound is real anyway: frontopolar MEAN AMPLITUDE, not variance ---
CSP features are log-variance, which is nearly blind to a steady DC shift --
and a sustained gaze deviation IS a steady DC shift. So section 4 undertests
the confound. Swapping the feature, not the channels, is what finds it.
  0.0-1.0 s (cue period         ): 86.7% (+/- 8.3%), p <= 0.001
  1.0-2.0 s (matched to headline): 62.2% (+/- 20.6%), p =  0.0939

Frontopolar asymmetry, mean(Fp1+AF7+AF3) - mean(Fp2+AF8+AF4), in microvolts:
  0.0-1.0 s: left cues +11.89 uV, right cues -12.99 uV  (Welch t = +7.71, p = 3.7e-09)
  1.0-2.0 s: left cues  -2.99 uV, right cues  +2.43 uV  (Welch t = -1.51, p = 0.14)
  0.0-4.0 s: left cues  +2.83 uV, right cues  -2.58 uV  (Welch t = +5.10, p = 7.6e-06)

The sign flips with the cue side and the effect is largest in the CUE window,
on the frontopolar left-minus-right contrast -- the electrodes nearest the
eyes. (Each side is a 3-channel average, Fp1/AF7/AF3 against Fp2/AF8/AF4, so
this statistic cannot attribute the effect to any individual electrode.)
That is the signature of the eyes moving to the target, not of sensorimotor
cortex.
Note the 1-2 s row reverses polarity: that row is not significant (p = 0.14),
so it is noise, and quoting it as a real reversal would be reading a coin flip.
EEGMMIDB has no EOG channels and this pipeline has no ICA, so this bounds the
confound; it does not remove it. n = 1 subject -- this is a flag, not a rate.
