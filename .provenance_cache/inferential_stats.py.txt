4. SUBJECT x METHOD INTERACTION -- TWO TESTS THAT DISAGREE BY DESIGN
==========================================================================
THE TRAP THIS SECTION EXISTS FOR. Two statistics near 13 have been quoted
for this question, one a chi-square on 19 df and one a Tukey 1-df F, and
their p-values are four orders of magnitude apart. They are not the same
test and neither is a typo. Both are computed here, from the same arrays.

--- A. homogeneity of the per-subject CSP-vs-MDM-motor difference, 19 df ---
  mean difference        -2.6 points
  win / loss / tie       9 / 8 / 3
  Cochran Q, per-arm binomial  chi2 = 13.58 on 19 df, p = 0.808
  Cochran Q, pooled binomial   chi2 = 13.33 on 19 df, p = 0.821
  contingency chi2 on the 20x2 correct-counts table: chi2 = 5.39 on 19 df, p = 0.999
  ASSUMPTION MADE EXPLICIT: the two Cochran forms differ only in whether
  the per-subject variance is estimated per arm or from the pooled rate.
  The contingency form treats the 20 subjects as independent samples of a
  common success rate, which is a different null and gives a different df
  interpretation.
  THE FIGURE THIS SECTION PRODUCES for the claim as worded is the pooled
  Cochran form: chi2 = 13.33 on 19 df, p = 0.821.
  Consistency arithmetic on the pair quoted in the documents, which is
  not a measurement: chi2.sf(13.0, 19) = 0.839, so
  the published statistic and its published p-value agree with each other.
  The variance model behind that statistic is stated nowhere, so this
  section reproduces its family rather than its digits, and the value a
  document should quote is the one printed two lines above.

--- B. Tukey 1-df non-additivity across all five pipelines ---
  F = 13.4627 on 1 and 75 df, p = 0.0005
  leave out MDM-64    F =   1.17, p = 0.2847
  leave out TSLR-64   F =  13.81, p = 0.0005
  leave out MDM-mot   F =  11.47, p = 0.0013
  leave out TSLR-mot  F =  12.91, p = 0.0007

--- which test answers the question, and why ---
  The claim at stake is 'no method dominates per subject' -- i.e. whether
  the CHOICE of the better method depends on the subject. Test A asks
  exactly that for one named pair, on the scale the claim is made on, and
  its 19 df match the 20-subject design. Test B asks whether a
  multiplicative fan exists across FIVE arms at once, one degree of
  freedom, and it is dominated by whichever arm has the widest per-subject
  spread. The leave-one-out rows above show the F collapsing when the
  64-channel MDM arm is dropped, which identifies that arm as the source.
  TEST A IS THE APPROPRIATE ONE for the claim as worded. Test B is a
  correct test of a different hypothesis.

--- what the null does NOT license ---
  sd of the per-subject difference  8.60 points
  MDE at 80% power, noncentral t    5.68 points
  A non-significant homogeneity test on 20 subjects cannot distinguish
  'no interaction' from 'an interaction this design cannot see'. The
  defensible wording is that no interaction is DETECTABLE here.
  argmax pipeline per subject: {'MDM-mot': 4, 'CSP+LDA': 8, 'MDM-64': 3, 'TSLR-64': 3, 'TSLR-mot': 2}
  A varying argmax is what an additive model with noise produces anyway,
  so the argmax spread is not itself evidence of an interaction.

6. RUNG 10 -- McNEMAR ON THE WITHIN-SUBJECT COMPARISON
==========================================================================
NO ARRAY ON DISK HOLDS THIS. McNemar needs the discordant pair counts,
which need the two models' PER-TRIAL predictions on the same folds.
eegnet_compare.py scores its folds and discards the predictions, so this
section re-runs experiment A to recover them: same 45 trials, same
StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=42), same seed, same model.
THE SEEDING ORDER IS COPIED, NOT APPROXIMATED. eegnet_compare.py seeds
once and lets cross_val_score clone across folds, so the five folds do
not restart from the same RNG state. Reseeding per fold changes four of
the five folds and moves the EEGNet count by several trials, which is
enough on its own to make a McNemar table irreproducible.

  CSP + LDA correct     41/45 = 91.1%
  EEGNet correct        37/45 = 82.2%
  EEGNet - CSP          -8.9 points
  EEGNet predicted class counts [21, 24] (true [21, 24])

--- the 2x2 agreement table ---
  both correct            34
  CSP only  (b)           7
  EEGNet only (c)         3
  neither correct         1
  McNemar exact, two-sided, binomial on 10 discordant pairs
  McNemar p = 0.344
  ASSUMPTION: exact binomial rather than the chi-square approximation,
  because 10 discordant pairs is far too few for the asymptotic form.

  THE MARGINAL COUNTS DO NOT DETERMINE THE SPLIT. Any (b, c) with
  b - c = 4 is consistent with 41/45 against 37/45, and the p-value
  depends on which one it is, not on the difference. The maximally nested
  split, b = 4 and c = 0, would give p = 0.125; the
  measured split of b = 7, c = 3 gives p = 0.344. Deriving a
  McNemar p from two accuracies alone is arithmetic on an assumption about
  agreement that the predictions themselves settle.
  What this does NOT show: a non-significant McNemar on 45 trials leaves
  the direction of the difference undecided. Only the sign of b - c is
  consistent with the point estimate; its magnitude is not established.
  DETERMINISM CAVEAT: MPS kernels are not bit-reproducible, so the EEGNet
  half of this table can shift by a trial or two between runs.

7. RUNG 10 -- THE BATCHNORM ACTIVATION-SCALE DEFICIT, MEASURED
==========================================================================
NO ARRAY ON DISK HOLDS THIS EITHER. No persisted artefact in this repo
records a per-layer activation standard deviation, so the deficit figure
has never been produced by a script. This section measures it directly:
forward hooks on every BatchNorm2d, one batch in a no-grad forward pass,
dropout disabled so the only thing varying is scale.

HOW THE MEASUREMENT IS DEFINED. BatchNorm divides by sqrt(var + eps). If
the input variance is far below eps the divisor is essentially sqrt(eps),
so the stage's output lands sqrt(eps)/sigma too small instead of at unit
scale. eps = 0.001, so sqrt(eps) = 0.0316.

11. WHERE THIS RUN AND THE PROSE DO NOT AGREE
==========================================================================
Reconciling the documents is a separate pass, so this section names the
disagreements rather than resolving them. In each case the value a
document should carry is the one this file prints in the section named.

  section 6, McNemar on the within-subject comparison
      the reproduced per-trial predictions give a discordant split that is
      not the maximally nested one, so the exact test lands well above the
      value obtainable from the two accuracies alone. A McNemar p derived
      from marginal accuracies is arithmetic on an assumption, and the
      predictions contradict that assumption here.

  section 7, the end-to-end activation-scale deficit
      the measured deficit at the logits is roughly two orders of magnitude,
      not the single-digit multiplier the 31.6x-per-stage recovery model
      implies. The first-BatchNorm deficit, by contrast, reproduces the
      figure the documents state as established.

  section 8, the training-margin shortfall
      computed against the measured deficit the shortfall is two orders of
      magnitude rather than the small factor the assumed recovery model
      gives, and the two definitions of 'travel' differ by about 2x, so a
      margin quoted without naming its definition cannot be checked.

  section 4, the subject x method interaction
      the homogeneity test this file computes lands near p = 0.82, not near
      the p the documents carry. The two are the same family of test with a
      different variance model, and no document states which model it used.

  section 9, the pre-cue control
      the interval is computed here for the first time and its upper bound
      sits above the cue-window CSP estimate the same rung calls
      significant, so 'the control passes' is not what these arrays support.
